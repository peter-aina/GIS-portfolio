PROJECT 01 / HAZUS · HURRICANE

Hurricane Michael: Damage and Loss Assessment, Florida Panhandle

Region
Gadsden, Leon, and Wakulla counties, Florida Panhandle. Census tract level. 1,967 sq mi, population 381,645.
Event
Hurricane Michael, October 10, 2018. Category 5 landfall near Mexico Beach with maximum sustained winds of 161 mph and minimum pressure of 919 mb.
Software
Hazus 7.0 · HURREVAC · ArcGIS Pro · QGIS · USGS 3DEP · Census TIGER/Line
Question
How was building damage and economic loss distributed across the three Panhandle counties most affected by Hurricane Michael, how much debris was generated, and how does the spatial pattern of physical loss overlap with social vulnerability and community resilience?
Data sources
Hazus 7.0 default hurricane scenario outputs for wind speed, peak gust, debris, damage, and loss. HURREVAC storm track for Hurricane Michael. CDC Social Vulnerability Index (2018). FEMA National Risk Index and Community Resilience Challenges Index. USGS 3DEP digital elevation model. Census TIGER/Line boundaries and roads.
Method
Defined the study area in Hazus 7.0 at the county level for Gadsden, Leon, and Wakulla. Loaded the Hurricane Michael advisory track from HURREVAC through the Hazus Create Scenario window and ran the analysis with default parameters. Symbolized MaxSustained, PeakGust, debris, damage, and loss outputs in ArcGIS Pro using manual interval classification with four classes. Clipped CDC SVI 2018 census tracts to the three study counties and mapped RPL_THEMES with five manual classes to match the CDC reference scheme.
Key finding
Total predicted economic loss across the three counties exceeded $32.7 million, with Gadsden County alone accounting for $18.2 million (56% of total loss) despite representing only 11.7% of the area's population, and residential buildings absorbed over 90% of all economic damage.
1.1 Hazard inputs

Wind field, peak gust, terrain context, and storm track. The physical hazard the loss model is built on. Maximum sustained wind reached 73 mph and peak gust 94 mph near Chattahoochee in Gadsden County, the closest point to the storm track.

PROJECT 02 / HAZUS · FLOOD

Hamilton County Flood Risk, Central Indiana

Region
Hamilton County, Indiana. Census tract level. 402 sq mi, population 387,036. Located in the Upper White River Watershed, directly north of Indianapolis.
Scenarios
Five riverine flood return periods (10, 25, 50, 100, and 500-year) plus Average Annualized Loss (AAL).
Software
Hazus 7.0 · ArcGIS Pro · QGIS · USGS 3DEP · Census TIGER/Line · USGS NHD
Question
How does expected flood damage and economic loss in Hamilton County scale across five return periods (10, 25, 50, 100, and 500-year), and how does the spatial pattern of risk overlap with socially vulnerable populations along the White River corridor?
Data sources
Hazus 7.0 with five course-provided flood depth grids (RPD10, RPD25, RPD50, RPD100, RPD500). CDC Social Vulnerability Index (2022). FEMA Resilience Analysis and Planning Tool (RAPT) and National Risk Index. USGS 3DEP digital elevation model. USGS National Hydrography Dataset. Census TIGER/Line boundaries and roads.
Method
Defined the study area in Hazus 7.0 for Hamilton County (FIPS 18057). Imported the five depth grids in the Create Flood Scenario window and assigned each to its matching return period, with the Average Annualized Loss option enabled. Symbolized the 500-year scenario first to set consistent classification breakpoints, then applied that same legend to all lower return periods so the maps could be read against one another. Clipped the CDC SVI 2022 census tracts to Hamilton County (FIPS 18057) and mapped RPL_THEMES with four manual classes.
Key finding
Total economic loss scales from $163.11 million in the 10-year scenario to $343.74 million in the 500-year scenario, with an Average Annualized Loss of $20.45 million concentrated along the White River corridor through Noblesville, Carmel, and Fishers.
2.1 Terrain, transportation, and social vulnerability

Elevation context, transportation and utility infrastructure, and the CDC Social Vulnerability Index that frames the risk story. Hamilton County's overall SVI of 0.032 is one of the lowest in Indiana, but a few tracts in Hortonville and Westfield show notably higher vulnerability.

SECTION 04

Workflow & methods

The general workflow behind the maps on this site, for visitors who want to understand the methodology before evaluating any single project.

01 / SOURCE

Source data

All maps are built from publicly available or institutionally licensed datasets: FEMA Hazus and the National Risk Index, Census ACS and PUMS files, CDC datasets including BRFSS and WONDER, and state and local geospatial portals for parcel, hydrography, and boundary layers.

02 / PROCESS

Processing

For the Hazus-based studies, the study area was defined at the county level and the analysis run with default building inventory and economic loss settings. Output layers were exported and brought into ArcGIS Pro for cartographic finishing. Where cross-scenario comparison was needed, the highest-magnitude scenario was symbolized first to fix consistent classification breakpoints across the series.

03 / CARTOGRAPHY

Cartography

Maps are finalized in ArcGIS Pro (or QGIS where appropriate). Symbology choices favor accessible color schemes and clear hierarchy: one primary variable per map, supporting context kept light.

04 / REPRODUCIBILITY

Reproducibility

Where possible, processing steps are scripted rather than clicked. Notebooks and scripts behind the maps are available on request.
